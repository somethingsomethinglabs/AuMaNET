# Bandwidth, scenarios and link observations

17 November 2026. Implemented as an assumption-only calculator in **Network → Plan bandwidth**, with a compact native-app HaLow observation button and accessible details. No firmware, device settings, identities, membership, saved history, Stop or microphone consent behavior changes. No physical operations were performed for this work. This does not close SW-18 or accept multi-hop service.

## Evidence and limits

The [12 November field analysis](field-radio-analysis.md) records four completed upload/download payload tests: 315.6/408.6 kbps at nominal 7 m and cached −46 dBm; 130.1/187.7 at 24 m and −61; 381.5/365.6 at 50 m and −49; 127.1/495.9 at 25 m without RSSI. Four attempts failed, including two near nominal 105 m with cached −74 dBm. These include phone Wi-Fi, gateways, HaLow, TCP and TLS. The GPS uncertainty, changing obstructions and unknown RF sample ages prohibit a useful distance/speed or RSSI/speed fit. Failed tests remain failures, not zero-speed successes.

Newer evidence takes precedence over the old SW-18 “not started” register: [15 November channel optimisation acceptance](reviews/20261115/channel-optimisation.json), also described in [radio settings](radio-settings.md), reports 17 completed tests across 12 channels at 1 MHz. Channel 42's repeated slower-direction median was 780 kbps, but first/final baseline drift was 37.4%, so no recommendation passed the 30% stability rule. These are distinct local conditions, not an improvement multiplier for the field records. The older analysis links to a now-missing `discovery/results` directory in this checkout; this work preserves that deletion and uses the surviving analysis/dated review rather than claiming raw records were revalidated. Local newer `dev/artifacts/channel-optimisation-device` and `native-link-benchmark` evidence exists, but is not a calibrated propagation survey.

**MHz describes channel width. kbps describes payload per second.** Neither wider channels nor stronger RSSI guarantees useful throughput. The calculator never converts RSSI, distance or channel width to speed. User-entered rates should come from repeated tests on a comparable path, retaining failures, asymmetric rates and context. Defaults 130/188 kbps approximate the slower completed field pair, not a percentile or worst case; outages below that range remain possible.

## Actual implementation and traffic budgets

Inspected sources: `VoiceLabEngine.java` and `NativeVoiceCoordinator.java` under `dev/mobile/android/app/src/debug/java/au/aumanet/app`; `GroupSession.java`, `GroupWire.java`, `GroupMessageRetry.java`, `GroupMessageMedia.java`, `GroupImageBulkBudget.java`, `GroupTextWire.java`, `LocationOwner.java` and `InternetTunnel.java` under `src/main/java/au/aumanet/app`; protocol radio/benchmark/message contracts; `dev/packages/core/src/radio-signal.ts`.

* Voice creates an individual WebRTC peer connection and audio sender per peer. Opus is required and each sender's `maxBitrateBps` is 24,000. This is an encoder ceiling, not measured constant traffic. RTP/RTCP multiplex over UDP host candidates, with DTLS-SRTP media protection in WebRTC. The configured receive jitter buffer maximum is 20 packets, not a guarantee of 20 ms latency. App code does not pin packet interval, Opus FEC/DTX, SRTP suite or audio retransmission policy; inspect negotiated SDP and RTP stats before treating them as actual. Do not assume every lost audio packet gets retransmitted. Wi-Fi MAC retry behavior is an additional driver/link effect.
* Use **20 ms packets as an assumption**, varying 10–60 ms. At 24 kbps, a 20 ms packet carries about 60 codec bytes. IPv4/UDP/RTP minimum headers are 20+8+12=40 bytes; SRTP authentication, RTP extensions, control/keepalive and implementation details add cost. The editable 80-byte allowance deliberately reserves more than those minimum headers: `24 + 80*8/20 = 56 kbps` per delivered voice stream. At 40 ms it is 40 kbps; at 10 ms it is 88 kbps, before shared headroom. These are conservative planning charges against bulk TCP payload goodput, **not exact wire accounting**. Large-transfer measurements already pay TCP/TLS/MAC overhead; applying another blanket TCP/MAC factor would double-count it. Here the full small-packet voice allowance intentionally errs conservative; a calibrated equivalent-goodput factor needs simultaneous RTP/bulk captures. FEC inside the encoder budget is not automatically another 24 kbps; any additional repair/control beyond the allowance must be reserved explicitly.
* For M members and T active talkers, there are `T*(M−1)` outgoing voice copies. A talker uploads `(M−1)*56 kbps`; a listener receives up to `min(T,M−1)*56 kbps`. Two-member turn-taking needs 56 kbps aggregate; simultaneous two-way speech needs 112. Four members with one talker need 168; two talkers 336; all four 672. No conference mixer or radio multicast gain is assumed. Local-node pairs and distinct contention domains may be cheaper; the current calculator conservatively charges every copy against one shared domain.
* Location requests native fixes at a nominal 1 s minimum; the group publishes changed fixes via its tick, coalesces queued locations by source and relays through the owner. Provider availability and background policies can reduce cadence. The calculator's **10 s GPS interval is a proposed workload, not an app setting**. At an assumed 500 bytes/update and fanout `M*(M−1)`, two phones use 0.8 kbps at 10 s, or 8 kbps at 1 s; ten phones use 36 or 360 kbps. Envelope size includes a conservative serialization/security allowance, not a measured packet capture. Aggregate fanout conservatively bounds owner relaying; topology-specific traversal counts need a traffic matrix.
* Text uses signed bounded envelopes over authenticated TCP/TLS group control. Basic text limit is 240 UTF-8 bytes, extended text 2,048 where supported. A planning allowance of 1,000 bytes per group text copy, two texts/minute across the group, costs 0.267 kbps for two members or 2.4 kbps for ten. Increase envelope size for long text/recipient lists. Automatic message attempts are bounded at five with retry delays 10/30/60/120 seconds; TCP also retransmits. Reliable delivery can therefore be late through an outage; capacity alone cannot promise delivery time.
* Photos are JPEG up to 1,048,576 bytes and 2,048 pixels/edge. Chunks are 4,096 bytes and encoded as base64 in JSON, adding about 33% before envelope/control. Bulk slots are coalesced at 250 ms idle, 500 ms voice-joined and 2,000 ms active voice. They are shared with other bulk work, with requests, responses, storage sync, RTT and retries adding delays. Ideal chunk pacing ceilings are 131.1/65.5/16.4 kbps of original image bytes. For 0.25 decimal MB, 62 chunks imply at least roughly 15.25/30.5/122 seconds from first to last slot, even with unlimited link rate; fetch/control may consume more slots. The displayed capacity floor excludes this pacing and base64, with that limitation visible beside the result. It must not be quoted as expected app completion time.
* Shared internet uses a consumer VPN/SOCKS bridge and donor-bound mobile sockets. TCP data is framed (4-byte lengths, up to 4,096-byte reads) over secure peer streams. UDP is also framed through those streams, so loss can cause head-of-line blocking. Existing internet encryption remains inside that transport where used. The extra 10% tunnel allowance is editable, not a benchmarked constant; short writes, nested encryption, VPN CPU, remote RTT, quotas and donor uplink can dominate. No donation starts from this estimator.

Primary references checked 17 November 2026: [Opus specification, RFC 6716](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc6716/) defines a broad codec range, not this app's measured traffic; [Opus RTP format, RFC 7587](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7587.html) describes packetization and FEC/DTX negotiation; [RTP, RFC 3550](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3550.html) defines headers/control and does not guarantee QoS; [WebRTC media transport, RFC 8834](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8834.html) covers secure RTP transport and feedback; [TLS 1.3, RFC 8446](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8446.html) defines record framing/protection. None establishes the negotiated packet layout of the installed app.

## Minimum, target and comfortable service

These are **proposed engineering screening budgets**, conditional on available service throughout the operation. Decimal units: 1 kbps=1,000 bit/s; 1 MB=1,000,000 bytes. They are not release requirements or safety guarantees. Workloads share airtime; do not allocate the full measured upload and full measured download simultaneously on a half-duplex radio.

| Use case | Minimum workable | Target | Comfortable | Quality condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Two phones, text and GPS | 2–5 kbps for short text and 10–30 s updates | 10–20 kbps, including 1 s GPS allowance | 30–50 kbps gives bursts/recovery room | Text screening: each within 10 s; aim <2 s. GPS age target ≤2 update intervals; stale beyond that, explicit unavailable during outage. |
| Two phones, one talker, current Opus ceiling | About 80 kbps with room around 56 kbps stream | 100–150 kbps | 200+ kbps for two-way speech/bursts | Mouth-to-ear minimum ≤400 ms; target ≤150 ms. Proposed jitter ≤60/30/20 ms and loss ≤3/1/0.5% for minimum/target/comfortable. Measure directional windows, late packets and burst loss. |
| Four members, one / two talkers | About 240 / 480 kbps before other workloads | 300 / 600 kbps | 500 / 1,000 kbps | Same voice checks; actual peer fanout and simultaneous talkers must be tested. All four speaking needs roughly 960 kbps with 30% headroom alone. |
| 0.25 MB photo, idle | 50–100 kbps: wire-capacity lower bound 20–40 s before encoding/pacing | 200–300 kbps | 500+ kbps helps concurrent work | No hard jitter requirement; allow retry/outage recovery. App pacing prevents speed alone delivering arbitrarily fast photos. |
| 10 MB online map download | 0.1 Mbps: ≥13.3 min before overhead | 1 Mbps: ≥80 s | 5 Mbps: ≥16 s | Prefer prepare offline; ordinary offline display needs no radio throughput. Tiles/imports vary in size; no fixed “map bandwidth.” |
| Modern browsing, 3 MB example | 0.25–0.5 Mbps: ≥96–48 s, selective/light sites | 2–5 Mbps: ≥12–4.8 s | 10+ Mbps: ≥2.4 s | These exclude DNS, handshake, rendering, requests and donor contention. Low RTT and reliable donor required. Heavy pages/video may be impractical. |

The delay goal is motivated by [ITU-T G.114](https://www.itu.int/rec/T-REC-G.114), which warns that interactive tasks suffer well below a 400 ms one-way planning bound. The jitter/loss numbers above are local trial hypotheses, not ITU guarantees. RTT/2 is not measured mouth-to-ear delay and is unreliable on asymmetric paths. With no measured latency/jitter/loss, the UI only says the **bandwidth arithmetic fits**. A one-second outage clips speech regardless of average throughput; a 5–30 s outage can leave old positions and queued messages. Track longest outage and recovery time, not just mean loss. Clocked speech fixtures/human listening, 10 text deliveries and position age under mixed load are required.

The 3 MB web example is a rounded scenario, grounded in the multi-megabyte scale of [HTTP Archive's page-weight report](https://httparchive.org/reports/page-weight?end=latest&lens=top1k&start=2024_05_01&view=list), whose mobile median is about 2.6 MB in the selected series, not a universal page size. Use 0.5–10 MB sensitivity and larger sizes for media-heavy sites. [Chrome's performance guidance](https://developer.chrome.google.cn/docs/devtools/performance-insights?hl=en) uses 2.5 s for good LCP; full-page transfer time and LCP are different metrics. A high-rate link cannot guarantee that rendering target.

## Reproducible estimator

`dev/app/src/lib/bandwidth-scenario.ts` is pure arithmetic, with all inputs, formulas, result ranges and limitations exported as `aumanet.bandwidth-scenario.v1` JSON. A [default example export](bandwidth-scenario-example.json) is included. No location, identities or stored observations are included. No probes or consent changes occur. Inputs remain local to the dialog instance; they are not voice, GPS or radio configuration controls. Export offers a browser download and selectable JSON fallback; Android file-saving behavior has not been physically verified.

Let G=min(upload,download), H=scenario hops, R=hops already represented in G, C=additional contention fraction, S=headroom fraction and U=chosen sensitivity. Shared capacity bounds are `G*(R/H)*(1−C)*(1−S)*(1±U)`. `R≤H` prevents extrapolating an improved single-hop rate from an untested mesh. H/R is an **assumed airtime penalty**, not a validated routing model; use actual end-to-end goodput on the same path with H=R to avoid charging those hops twice. It assumes shared-channel serial forwarding with no spatial reuse; hidden nodes, unequal hop rates and relay overhead can be worse. A physical traffic matrix is needed for a defensible multi-domain budget.

Voice demand is `(codecKbps + overheadBytes*8/packetMs)*T*(M−1)`. GPS is `gpsEnvelopeBytes*8/1000/gpsSeconds*M*(M−1)`. Text is `textEnvelopeBytes*8/1000*groupTextsPerMinute/60*(M−1)`. Remaining capacity is `max(0,capacity−voice−GPS−text−otherKbps)`. Transfer floor is `sizeMB*8000/remainingKbps`; no residual capacity exports null, never a finite promise. Internet capacity is conservatively `min(residual,donorUpload,donorDownload)/(1+tunnelAllowance)`, using the weaker donor direction rather than assuming unlimited return traffic. Other consumers must be included in `otherKbps`.

Use low/high direction-specific measurements by setting central rates and a broad sensitivity bound. Bounds are **not confidence intervals** and never include every possible outage. The display always labels confidence low. “Fits assumed range” means demand ≤lower capacity, “Marginal” means it fits only the upper bound; “Exceeds” means it exceeds even that. All remain conditional on delay/loss testing. Avoid a green “voice ready” badge based on this output.

Worked default: 130/188 kbps, ±30%, 10% contention and 30% reserve gives 57.33–106.47 kbps. Two members, one talker, GPS every 10 s and two texts/minute consume 57.07 kbps; arithmetic narrowly fits, leaving about 0.26–49.40 kbps. It has effectively no margin at the low end beyond the already reserved headroom. A 0.25 MB photo floor spans about 40.5 s–127 min before pacing/encoding. Two simultaneous talkers do not fit even the upper bound. Four-member voice also fails this slower-field scenario.

For the faster 315.6/408.6 pair with identical assumptions, capacity is about 139–258 kbps: one four-member talker (168 kbps plus 4.8 kbps GPS and 0.8 kbps text) is marginal, and two talkers exceed it. This is a sanity check of workload scaling, not evidence that four phones were tested. Even the 780 kbps nearby channel result would not establish comfortable general browsing or stable multi-hop voice.

## Compact quality presentation

The native top bar shows a neutral `HaLow −67 dBm`, `HaLow stale` or `HaLow unknown` button. It carries **no bars, quality percent, SNR, speed or location claim**. Detail opens directional node observations, read/report age, provenance and “RF sample age unknown,” through the existing `radioSignalView`. The 30 s threshold is UI cache-read/report residence staleness, not proof of a fresh RF sample. Authenticated but stale numbers remain historical details; group errors suppress the compact number. Phone-to-node attachment is separate and has no fabricated RSSI. End-to-end service is explicitly unassessed, with a link to the existing consented test flow. No live quality probe was added.

Future measured service presentation should use separate peer/path/donor scopes and metric windows: receiver/transmitter, node boot/config, observation age, valid sample count, RTT distribution, loss interval, outage and residual upload/download. Prefer a compact “Service: tested / aged / unknown” description with time and peer over repurposing a direct-hop RSSI as mesh quality. Show historical benchmark configuration/age in details; do not automatically reuse an old success after a path, peer, boot or channel change. Current contracts do not supply that complete live health record, so this increment does not invent it.

## Untested locations and next trials

For an unvisited place, report a scenario envelope (“if the path sustains X–Y kbps, this workload may fit”), with **no geographic guarantee**. A future RF path tool needs terrain/clutter profiles, buildings/wall materials, antenna heights/polarization/patterns, cable losses, lawful EIRP, receiver sensitivity by actual mode, interference/noise and a calibrated fading margin. [ITU-R P.1812-8](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.1812/en) is a current path-specific propagation reference; its applicability and inputs must be assessed before using it for a short indoor/near-ground path. Path loss alone still does not determine application throughput. No synthetic RF map is implemented.

Follow [field QA](field-qa-checklist.md) with nearby A–B–A baselines and three repeats per geometry, retaining failed/partial tests. Record installed app/firmware identities, width/channel, mounting, antenna orientation/height, exact node positions and both phone attachment conditions. Measure both directions, delay distribution, loss bursts, jitter, longest outage and recovery under text/GPS, one/two talkers, concurrent photos and donor traffic. Verify actual Opus packetization/repair and image pacing against captures/counters. Sweep GPS cadence and 2/4/10-member fanout. A third node with direct path excluded and forwarding phone absent is required for relay capacity; two-node results cannot pass it. Hardware acceptance and installations remain coordinated with the onboarding chat; none were requested or performed here.

Validation commands and synthetic visual evidence are recorded by `dev/tests/bandwidth-scenario.test.mjs` and `dev/tests/bandwidth-scenario-browser-verification.mjs`. Their outputs are software/synthetic evidence, not RF, DHCP, voice acoustics or installed-app physical acceptance.

## App tool collection

**Network → Tools & checks** and **Settings → Tools & checks** group connection testing, bandwidth planning, radio settings/channel comparisons, voice setup and diagnostics. The collection explains the sequence: confirm device/version/attachment, measure both directions, plan the mixed workload, then trial delivery/position age/voice/photos and recovery. It records no invented pass state and opening it authorizes no probes or device changes.

**Test connection → Test history → Plan use cases with these rates** copies a completed test's upload/download into planning only after an explicit action. Incomplete/failed results are not offered as usable rates. The estimator labels the source as a historical test with its time; workload defaults and quality checks remain assumptions. Scenario JSON retains the original starting rates/time separately from edited inputs. Entering fresh planning from the tools collection clears that historical seed. A single successful test remains insufficient to establish a dependable lower bound; repeat it and retain failures.

`node dev/tests/tools-hub-browser-verification.mjs` passed synthetic navigation, explicit 80/80 kbps fixture handoff, fresh-planning reset, no arm/start commands, and 320/390/768 px overflow checks. The existing estimator browser suite still passes, and Svelte check reports zero errors/warnings. Hardware installation and shared acceptance are separate coordinated work; this change does not operate phones or nodes.
