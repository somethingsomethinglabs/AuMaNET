# AuMaNET logo council

6 November 2026. Concept archive; option 1 has since been adopted in the application and project branding. See the [active identity and master assets](../../brand/README.md).

## Owner selection

**Selected: Option 1 — Connected ridge**, confirmed by the project owner on 6 November 2026. This takes precedence over the council's recommendation below. Preserve the simple connected ridge, forest green and AuMaNET wordmark shown in [the selected board](connected-ridge.png). Production vector assets and application/Android/handbook branding are now maintained in [the active identity](../../brand/README.md).

## Brief

Keep the current identity's simplicity and forest green, but move closer to AuMaNET's purpose: helping a crew stay connected on Australian off-grid trips through local voice, messages and member positions. Internet is optional. The symbol should suit an app header, a small app icon and a one-colour device sticker. Do not attempt to depict every feature.

## Council

Three independent agents contributed as brand strategist, identity designer and product/user advocate, followed by a challenge round sharing their positions.

- **Brand strategist:** initially suggested a ridgeline with equal endpoint/peak nodes. After challenge, dropped the dots as unnecessary clutter; prefers a distinctive continuous ridge, with paired trail as the alternative.
- **Identity designer:** recommends a continuous connected ridge without dots. Preserves continuity while using one connected gesture. Warns that a rotational crew loop can resemble recycling or synchronisation; paired trail may imply only two travellers.
- **Product/user advocate:** prefers paired trail because people staying together is the benefit, while mesh is the mechanism. A mountain risks remaining a generic outdoors symbol. Suggested a sheltered/interlocking link as another direction and warned against generic networking diagrams.

Two members prefer the ridge; one prefers paired trail. This is creative judgment, not human recognition research or a trademark clearance. The coordinator retains a non-rotational open-link experiment to test connection more directly without the rejected recycling motif.

## Visual directions

- **Connected ridge:** one compact continuous angular band, broad uneven peaks and a memorable joining valley; no endpoint dots. Familiarity and terrain continuity. Risk: still reads primarily as mountains.
- **Paired trail:** two substantial rounded paths sharing a subtle bridge/central space. Togetherness and movement. Risk: roads, pause symbol, or only two people.
- **Open link:** two interlocking open rounded forms enclosing a shared clearing, with no central hub. Connection and mutual support. Risk: generic chain/infinity symbolism.

All use one flat forest green (#164b32) on white, ample whitespace and the exact AuMaNET wordmark. Each image explores one direction only. App screenshots are supplied as brand reference, not as a target to redesign the interface. Built-in Image Gen is used for raster concept boards; these are not final production vector masters. No claim of proven 24px legibility, manufacturing readiness or trademark uniqueness is made.

## Generation record

No other logos, mockup scenery, slogan, gradients, shadows or device frame. Prompts and final concept files are saved alongside this note after generation.

During the exploration stage, application assets were left unchanged. The owner subsequently chose option 1, now implemented through the active identity linked above.

## Rendered boards and final council critique

The images appeared in chat in this order, which defines the selection numbers:

1. [Connected ridge](connected-ridge.png)
2. [Paired trail](paired-trail.png)
3. [Open link](open-link.png)

All three were produced with built-in Image Gen and inspected visually. The wordmark reads AuMaNET, each board repeats one symbol rather than mixing concepts, and the green/white direction is retained. These are raster explorations; exact flat colour, repeatable geometry and final vector construction still need refinement.

After inspecting the actual boards, **two council members prefer Paired trail; one prefers Connected ridge**. This differs from the initial concept vote and reflects the rendered results.

- **Strategist:** Paired trail now leads for its uncomplicated silhouette and two participants moving together. Tighten the bends and diagonal span to reduce generic road/water associations. Ridge still reads primarily as mountains; Open link feels like a chain or stylised S.
- **Identity designer:** retains Connected ridge for outdoor recognition and continuity, while acknowledging that its closed baseline does little to communicate connection. Explore a lighter stroke and deliberate baseline opening. Finds the other marks more generic.
- **User advocate:** Paired trail feels friendlier and closer to a shared journey. Reduce vertical sweep, align endpoints more deliberately and preserve a distinctive central gap. Open link is more corporate; ridge remains an outdoors badge.

Coordinator recommendation: refine Paired trail, keeping the two substantial forms and one-colour simplicity; test whether a compact shared gap can make it more recognisable without adding literal radio symbols. Actual 24px legibility and sticker tests have not been performed. The owner may choose any of the three directions or request a new exploration.
