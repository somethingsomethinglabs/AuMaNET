How to read this guide

Captured reference means the approved prototype as it exists. Adoption guidance means an implementation rule or improvement proposed here, such as native touch targets and darker secondary text. These are labelled rather than silently altering the reference.

Use the supplied artwork

  • Keep the spelling AuMaNET and preserve the proportions.
  • Do not redraw, stretch, rotate, add gradients or add a tagline to the lockup.
  • Use the mark alone where the product name is already established.
  • Keep the logo for launch, identity and About surfaces. Task screens retain their task title.

Adoption guidance

  • Clear space: at least one quarter of mark height on all sides.

Spacing & shape

Use 16px screen padding, 12px control gaps and 14px between sections. Existing exceptions are captured in tokens.json. List groups use 14px corners; cards 16px; sheets 24px upper corners. Quiet dividers use 1px line colour. Avoid decorative elevation on routine cards.

Icons & imagery

The supplied 51-icon set uses a 24×24 viewBox, 1.6px round strokes, 20px default size and 22px navigation size. Keep names on navigation and accessible labels on icon-only actions. Schematic maps are reference imagery, not real cartography.

Contrast is an adoption task

The captured muted colour has 3.82:1 contrast on paper. For ordinary small text, this guide targets at least 4.5:1 and proposes #626D60 (4.87:1). Keep the screenshots as evidence; use the proposed secondary-text token when integrating and verify every actual surface. Do not claim the prototype is accessibility-certified.

03 / LAYOUTS

The screen belongs to the task.

The application owns the viewport. A compact app bar and persistent navigation surround one screen. The phone bezel and rotate button belong to the preview, not to the shipped interface.

App bar 68px minimum
Active screen flexible space · one vertical scroll owner
Bottom navigation 66px reference

Portrait

390×844 reference. Use 16px screen inset. Messages scrolls its history; the composer stays outside it. Map and Voice use the full available screen. Tools and forms scroll within the middle region.

Landscape & keyboard

844×390 reference. App bar and navigation reduce to 54px; Voice becomes two columns. Rotation keeps the live session. For a real soft keyboard, resize the usable viewport and keep the composer above it; add native safe areas and verify on devices.

Messages

The conversation is the screen, with a stable place to type.

  • One scrollable history. The composer and navigation stay outside it.
  • Preserve drafts across navigation and rotation. A failed save keeps the draft and shows Retry.
  • Use sender names, time and device-storage status. Never imply a human read receipt.
  • Keep a reader’s scroll position; follow new content only when already at the latest message.

Voice

Participants, current state and deliberate speaking controls.

  • Join muted. Enabling the microphone is a separate choice.
  • A fresh press starts hold-to-talk; release, backgrounding or interruption ends it.
  • Mute, received sound and Leave are distinct controls.
  • Show the persistent voice strip on other destinations. Applying voice settings mutes input.

Map

Map-first space with contextual controls and inspection sheets.

  • The map fills the available screen, with controls overlaid on its edges.
  • Private acquisition and published location are separate actions.
  • Label last-known positions, age and accuracy. Do not turn stale fixes into current ones.
  • Inspect people or coordinates in a sheet; return to the same map view.

People & Tools

Short rows and direct destinations, with detail on demand.

  • Group destinations by Network, Devices and Tools.
  • A row is a destination; a switch changes one explicit setting.
  • Show the current value or status as trailing metadata, not a paragraph.
  • Use a back action for secondary screens. Put deeper explanations in disclosures.

Sheets

  • Open from the relevant screen, with a concise title and close action.
  • Keep background controls inert and contain focus. Escape closes; focus returns to the triggering control.
  • Put explanations next to decisions. Essential consequences must remain visible.
  • Use scrolling within a tall sheet, without adding independent scrolling cards inside it.

Touch & text adoption

  • The captured primary controls are generally 44px high. Some switches and secondary controls are smaller.
  • Give native hit regions at least 48×48 logical units, including icon controls and switches.
  • Respect system text scaling and reflow; do not freeze every reference dimension at large text sizes.
  • Test focus visibility, keyboard order, screen-reader names and announcements on the actual platform.
05 / STATES & LANGUAGE

Say what happened. Keep the next step clear.

Write labels and state, not website copy. Necessary consent, uncertainty and recovery information stays at the moment it matters.

StateExampleRequired treatment
EmptyNo results yetName the missing item and give one useful next action.
OfflineConnection: offlineRetain saved history, drafts and maps; disable only unavailable actions.
PendingConnecting / PreparingDescribe the current operation and provide Cancel when possible.
ActiveVoice · mutedShow active service state beyond its owning screen.
FailedNot saved · RetryKeep input intact; provide an inline recovery action.
UnknownUnknown · check deliveryHold the original intent. Inspect status before repeating.
StaleLast known · age · accuracyKeep historical context visible without claiming freshness.
DeniedPermission unavailableExplain the needed capability at the attempted action; support retry.
DestructiveRemove member / Clear historyName the consequence and the exact scope before confirming.

Use

  • Messages · Voice · Map · People · Tools
  • Join voice, muted · Enable mic · Mute · Leave
  • Location off · Find my position · Share location
  • Not saved · Retry · Unknown · Recheck
  • Short verbs naming the exact action or consequence.

Keep out of task screens

  • Promotional slogans and oversized editorial introductions.
  • Repeated descriptions of what the app is for.
  • Claims that saved means read, or that Wi-Fi means all services work.
  • Hidden permission changes triggered by navigation.
  • Generic success messages when the outcome is still unknown.

State contracts to preserve

  • Navigation and refresh do not send messages, join voice or publish location.
  • Voice input, received audio, private location, location publication and shared internet are separate choices.
  • Save the draft before accepting a send. A failed write retains the draft and leaves the outbox unchanged.
  • Cancelling invalidates late callbacks. An unknown maintenance outcome blocks conflicting work until rechecked.
  • Keep the operation’s original identity, recipients and observed conditions; do not rewrite history with later information.

07 / HANDOVER

Keep the design source independent.

This pack documents the revised clean-room prototype. The original application's UI, code, screenshots, style sheets and evidence inventory were not consulted to create it.

1. Start with this package

The approved screenshot library, rules and tokens are the visual authority. The neutral functional brief remains the capability contract.

2. Keep design input separate

Do not feed original-app screenshots, styles or existing navigation into further design decisions. Record any new decisions in an addendum.

3. Map functionality to destinations

Inventory actions and state contracts first. Implement them within these layouts without importing old visual structure.

4. Implement the shared shell first

App bar, five destinations, one scroll owner, keyboard-aware composer, sheets and safe areas precede decorative refinement.

5. Verify the hard states

Check failure, offline, stale data, unknown outcomes, cancellation and recovery as well as the happy path.

6. Review against matched captures

Use the same route, state and viewport. Compare geometry and interaction, then test actual devices, text scaling and assistive technology.

Acceptance for the implementation team

  • Five direct destinations with consistent back and current-tab behaviour.
  • One vertical scroll owner per screen. No page scroll around the conversation.
  • Composer visible above navigation and the real keyboard; rotation keeps drafts and current sessions.
  • Voice joins muted, releases hold on interruption and exposes Mute / Leave outside its screen.
  • Location is private until explicitly shared, with stale positions identified.
  • Sheets restore focus; every icon action is named; state is never conveyed by colour alone.
  • Small-phone, landscape, large-text, long-name, offline, denied-permission and unknown-outcome checks.
  • No original promotional page structure, sample-data selector or old/new implementation switch introduced into normal product screens.
What this pack does not establish

It is a design and interaction reference, not native acceptance. Radio, audio, GPS, firmware, VPN and peer operations are simulated. Native permissions, keyboard insets, platform icon masks, screen readers, text scaling and dark theme need implementation validation. The guide does not modify the production application.
